# Effects

Part of CLAUDE.md, moved out to keep every session's context small. Read it before working in this area; keep it current.

- **Effects that read far around a patch** (Light's long spikes and wide glows) make their result once over the whole image, a band a core (`SkissaPixels::wholeResult`), when their reach is 128 px or more: each patch made most of the image's shine again.
- **Effects on the canvas** (`KisStudioEffectOverlay`, `KisFilterManager::setInlineEditor`): any filter previews live on the layer with a small draggable glass panel; Enter keeps, Esc drops, the mask button keeps it editable. Double-clicking a filter mask's or filter layer's row (but its switches) opens the panel on that node's own settings (`KisStudioEffectOverlay::edit`, through `KisFilterManager::editInline`): the node previews itself, Enter is one undo step from the settings it had, Esc puts them back; Krita's dialog stays for what the panel can't edit (generators). Undecided, it is kept by anything that moves on (saving, a new layer, picking another layer, switching or closing the image), as color drops are; only Esc, ✕ or undoing past it drops it. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z step through its own settings, a gesture at a time, while it is open (`noteChange`, `stepBy`). It opens with the last applied settings (`KisFilterManager::lastUsedConfiguration`), reset shows while they differ from defaults. Picker tiles render with the same settings at the thumbnail's level of detail, each into its own device. The panel goes in the widest free stretch of canvas; its settings grow to 65% of the canvas's height before they scroll (`settingsRoom`; a fixed 560 px cut Drawing off on a 1440 px tall screen). While open, autosave waits (`autosaveHeld`) and a busy image is waited on quietly instead of Krita's modal "Waiting…". A preview may never write the real device outside a `KisTransaction`. An effect's settings widget that implements `KisEffectCanvasEditor` (found with `qobject_cast`) gets the pointer over the image and drags on it instead of the first-numbers drag. One with a middle (`canvasMiddle`: Lens, Radial blur) has it drawn on the canvas as a handle (`MiddleMark`, a child of the canvas widget following `sigCanvasStateChanged`, only as large as what it draws) under a crosshair; one with a sample of what it does (`canvasStreak`: Radial blur's streak) has that drawn by the middle, a fixed size on screen, ending in a knob that drags (`canvasStreakDragged`). The eye is instant both ways: it switches what the canvas shows, the picture as it was when the effect opened (`before`, through `KisOpenGLImageTextures::setShownInstead`) or the image, and the preview keeps following changes underneath; a canvas without OpenGL still cancels and reapplies, and so does a view zoomed out with instant preview, where the preview exists only at a level of detail (the image itself has no effect until it is applied, so the canvas made again from it showed none: the eye seemed to drop the effect).
- **Effects dropped where they act** (`KisStudioItemDrop`): an effect dragged out of the effects picker (`dragKey` `effect:`) opens on the layer that shows most where it lands on the canvas (`KisSelectLayerAction::layersAt`, the same pick as a click) or on the layer row it is dropped on (`KisNodeModel::dropMimeData`); inside a selection as any effect is. A brush dropped on the canvas is picked.
- **Effects, by what they do to a pixel** (the plan the owner agreed to): Color (by its own color), then Detail (one curve over feature size: blur, sharpen, high pass, noise reduction, with one-slider presets like Gaussian blur), Noise (white noise shaped by that same size curve), Lens (physical: dispersion, fringes, distortion, cos⁴ vignette, glow), Radial blur (zoom, spin, light rays), Drawing (difference of Gaussians: lines with ink, pencil, hatching, strokes, dots, a print screen, engraving or contours), Fewer colors (posterize, palette, dithering in OKLab, pixel art), Shape (the layer's distance field: outline, glow, shadow, bevel, grow), Light (bright parts scattering light: glow, star, streak, halation), Painting (anisotropic Kuwahara), Lens blur (out of focus through an aperture) and Replace color, plus shader effects. Krita's effects one of them does entirely are retired (`KisRetiredEffects::coveredBy`), and the dated or narrow ones are left out (`isLeftOut`: Wave, Raindrops, Small tiles, Round corners, the Embosses, Edge detection, Phong bump, Dodge, Burn, Tone/Gamut map, Fast color overlay, Color transfer, Height to normal map, Reset transparent): not in Find or the picker, still registered so files and masks render, and still in the classic Filter menu; favorites of left-out ones go; favorites of them become the new effect, and the keys of those it opens instead (Ctrl+M, Ctrl+U, Ctrl+L, Ctrl+B, from Krita's shortcut schemes) open it (`replacement`), set to do what the old one did where that takes a setting (`replacementSettings`: Halftone opens Drawing's screen, Pixelize Fewer colors' pixel art, Gradient map Color's map). Common uses of one effect are effects of their own with a slider or two (Blur, High pass, Smooth are Detail; Sharpen, Krita's unsharp by id, is retired into Detail, its keys and old ids opening Detail's Crisp, `replacementSettings`, and old masks still rendering), so the simple thing stays one click and one slider. Upstream Krita can't render a mask of a Skissa effect, except Blur, Sharpen and High pass, which are Krita's Gaussian blur, unsharp and gaussianhighpass by id and settings (Skissa makes their blurs and honours Krita's settings it doesn't show: a blur stretched one way, lightness only, threshold; Krita's own Unsharp and High pass aren't built, its Gaussian blur isn't registered; the `skissa_*` ids from before still open). Applied effects are plain pixels.
- **Color effect** (`plugins/filters/skissa_color`): curves of lightness, chroma and hue by each of them in OKLCH (the diagonal maps, the rest shift or scale; the hue axis is spaced as an HSV wheel, `axisOfHue`), red, green and blue curves, a white balance (von Kries in Oklab's cone responses, `Recipe`) and shadow and highlight tints as discs. Exposure, contrast, vibrance and saturation are curve shapes, not sliders (the owner's call). The lightness-by-lightness (tone) curve scales chroma with the lightness it gives, as an exposure does (Oklab scales both alike with the light), so Brighter doesn't wash colors out; the selective lightness curves (by chroma, by hue) leave chroma alone. Curves are monotone cubics (no overshoot); a curve by hue wraps and its ends are one point. Tiles of the layer under a few starting points (Auto stretches the lightness between the half-percent ends). A drag up or down on the image bends the shown curve at that pixel's value, read from a copy taken before the preview (`KisPaintDevice` copy, tiles shared); on a curve by hue it holds the curve a twelfth of a turn either way, so one hue changes alone. Settings files hold only what differs from the defaults. Integer images are kept in gamut by giving up chroma (`KoOklch::inGamut`), past white as a near-white is (`fitted`: cut channel by channel, pure white turned cyan beside a neutral 254), and white balance scales its gains so white itself stays inside (`m_whiteKept`), so paper takes the grays' cast; float ones keep what is past sRGB. Its last tab, Map, is the gradient map, applied before everything else: each pixel's Oklab lightness becomes the stops' color there, blended in Oklab or the nearest stop's (Steps), with Keep lightness (the map gives only hue and chroma) and Strength; looks as tiles (Picture: five stops of the layer's own colors by lightness, to edit from), the stops on `widgets/KisStudioGradientBar` over the layer's lightness, and a press on the image takes the stop at that pixel's lightness (or adds one) while a drag recolors it (up lighter, sideways around the hues). It retires Krita's Gradient map, whose panel stays for old masks. Under the curve, each of the Lightness, Chroma and Hue tabs has one row for the whole image at once, after the curves (`Settings::shift`: lightness added, chroma scaled, hue turned in degrees; `shift_lightness`, `scale_chroma`, `shift_hue`), Krita's HSV adjustment in OKLCH; the tab's right click resets it too. A curve that wraps keeps its two ends once among the turns it is sampled with (`KisStudioCurve::sampled`): kept twice, the smoothing met a flat step at each end and drew the straight hue-by-hue curve as an S.
- **Detail effect** (`plugins/filters/skissa_detail`): gain over feature size, as the image times the finest features' gain plus, for each radius where the gain changes, the image blurred to it times the change (`Plan`); so Blur, High pass and Sharpen are exactly one step, with Krita's radius (sigma 0.3 r + 0.3). Its curve is sampled every half octave from 1 to 1024 px. Blurs under sigma 4.5 are Krita's kernel; wider ones are made on a pyramid shared by every step (`Pyramid`: halved by a 1 5 10 10 5 1 binomial, the blur made exactly on the coarsest level where it spans two cells, enlarged by a cubic B-spline whose variance is taken off): within 1/40 of an 8-bit level of an exact Gaussian on a hard edge, the image's edge repeating as in Krita's blurs, and any size costs what a small one does. The edge-aware blur finds its two coefficients on the level's cells too. Clarity on 8 MP went from 45 s to under 3. What a curve takes of the largest shapes becomes middle gray (high pass); transparency only ever blurs, and where a step changes the colors and the transparency apart (sharpening a cut-out), the colors it adds are the visible ones around, its blur divided by the blurred alpha, times the pixel's own alpha (blurred with the clear pixels, they counted as black and lit a rim inside every edge). A smaller copy (a level of detail) scales the blur's sigma, not Krita's radius. Edges make each blur a guided filter on luma (Rec. 709 weights of the channels it blurs; epsilon (0.02 / edges)²). Beyond the image its edge repeats, as Krita's blurs do. A press on the image finds the size that stands out there (`busiestRadius`) and a drag up or down changes it alone. The curve editor is shared with Color (`widgets/KisStudioCurve`).
- **Noise effect** (`SkissaNoise` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`): white noise from a hash of the pixel and the seed (the same wherever and however often it is drawn, so patches meet), split into Detail's half-octave bands, each brought to unit spread (exactly, from the 1D kernels' overlaps) and to the strength the size curve gives it. Bands of large sizes are made on grids a power of two coarser and enlarged bilinearly, so clouds cost what grain does; those grids are anchored to the full image's pixels, so a preview at a lower level of detail shows the clouds the image gets. One field per target: lightness and color in Oklab (two fields), transparency, Displace (two fields: fine scatters, large wobbles). Midtones weighs lightness noise toward the middle tones; near black, and near white where the picture stops there, the noise stays within two spreads of the end (cut on one side it darkened whites and lifted blacks), so grain fades into paper white and solid black. The dice gives another seed. It replaces Random noise, Random pick and the fork's old Grain (`skissa_grain`, retired: Noise's Grain look does it).
- **Lens effect** (`SkissaLens` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`): what a camera lens does, in linear light, with every size a share of the half diagonal (so a look is the same on any image and at any level of detail). Fringes: each wavelength magnified by 1 + fringes × its dispersion (Cauchy, in units of the F and C lines), sampled at even steps of dispersion a pixel apart at the farthest corner (8 to 48) and weighted by the CIE 1931 fit turned into sRGB, negatives dropped, each channel's weights adding to one; nothing moves at the middle. Halo: red and blue (or green, below zero) blurred as if out of focus. Bulge: r (1 + k r²), a barrel keeping the farthest corner in place. Vignette: cos⁴. Glow: light moved, not made, to sizes an octave apart in equal shares, the large ones blurred on coarser grids made straight from what was read; in an image that stops at white what spreads is the light its whites stand for (`unclipOf`), so a white lamp blooms instead of dimming. Glow size shows only while there is a glow. Coverage is carried per channel, so a see-through layer's fringes stay inside what is there. A press on the image places the middle (`KisEffectCanvasEditor::canvasDragged` gets the point under the drag). It reads from anywhere in the frame, so its needed rect is huge and the coarse preview chooses its level by pixel count alone (`KisCoarseFilterPreview::level`). 24 MP: about 1 s for a fisheye, 2 s for Vintage. Nothing of Krita's is retired by it.
- **Radial blur** (`SkissaRadial` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`): zoom, spin (both at once a spiral) and light rays about a middle, in linear light. Each pixel averages along its streak, the orbit of one zoom-and-turn (a log spiral; rays for zoom alone, circles for spin alone), weighted by length and fading as a square from the pixel (`back` of it beyond the pixel for spin, so shapes stay put). Streaks are straight lines in log-polar coordinates, so each patch sums lines a fixed step of λ = θL − uA apart once (running sums of the picture times 1, s, s², in doubles: floats lost whole levels where a turn's long lines cancel; only the channels the look uses; samples stepped by a complex multiplication, a new step every octave of radius) and each pixel reads the two either side: a long streak costs what a short one does (8 MP in about 0.5 s, as Lens 0.4 s). The lines are the same in every patch (a whole number around a turn, coarser by powers of two where a patch's streaks reach less far), so patches meet within a few levels at hard edges. Picture is how much the picture itself streaks. Light: what shines as Light finds it (`SkissaPixels::shineOf`) past Threshold × the layer's brightest parts (`top`, its 99.5th percentile, found by the panel and kept in the settings), scaled so the brightest parts give their own color, averaged along the streaks and added as light (over clear pixels as opaque as it is bright); shapes in front cast shadows in the rays; an absolute threshold left night scenes without any. Shafts break the light into beams where it starts (smooth noise by angle, a dice for others), so rays show in open sky. A press on the image places the middle (it may lie past the edges) and a drag moves it; the streak drawn by the middle has its end as a knob: in zooms, around spins, the two sides shared as the effect shares them, so the knob stays under the pointer. Pulling streaks anywhere on the image with nothing drawn was tried and read as unintuitive.
- **Drawing** (`SkissaInk` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`, id `skissa_ink`; it was called Ink): the picture drawn by hand, its lines by the extended difference of Gaussians (Winnemöller's XDoG) along the picture's flow (Kang's FDoG), after Acerola's shader, with all its parts behind three choices. What is drawn, four rows that always show: Size (the blurs: Size and 1.6 × it), Lines (the sharpening, p up to 300% as a square; differences under 0.006 dropped, or 8-bit gradient steps show as stripes), Shading (a share of the layer, darkest first: the panel finds the lightness it reaches, `level`, kept in the settings, so a look reads alike on light and dark pictures), Flow (the structure tensor of Oklab, Sobel smoothed by 1.5 + Size, gives the direction along edges; the lines are smoothed along it by a line integral of a third of its sigma a step, then their edges by a pixel; the picture under the ink too, for Picture and Colors: Wash; each sample counts by how close its lightness is to the start's, none a tenth or more apart, `edgeRange`: smoothed regardless, eyes melted into blobs and a ring across a pen was smeared along the pen into hairs). How the shading is drawn, as tiles, each with its own settings (`Shading`): Solid (steps of hard ink), Soft (one threshold as soft as asked, and Grain: the paper's tooth, `toothOf`, catching the ink on its peaks first, a threshold of evenly spread heights, so the tone stays, never so soft that bare paper catches some or full ink leaves some: pencil, charcoal), Hatch (`hatchAt`: three directions, the second crossing; rows by rank, every fourth first, then every second, then all, each coming in thin and widening over a quarter of its range so rows taper where the shading ends; long strokes with small gaps, each a little off its row and tilted; covering as much as the picture is dark, `hatchCovers`: the first direction, then the crossing ones over it, then all pressed wider with shorter gaps until they close; filled by thirds of the darkness, mid-tones came out a third as dark as they should), Strokes (the XDoG paper's own hatching: white noise fixed to the image's pixels, filtered across the flow by a broad Gabor kernel into stripes Spacing apart and along it by a line integral into strokes Length long (`strokeAlong`, turning with the flow every third step; reading the nearest pixel instead of between pixels made them grainy, a step of two pixels dotted), `stripesOf`, made on a grid a fifth of a stripe apart once stripes are 10 px or wider (coarser read as blotchy), brought to unit spread around each place and cut where a normal quantile makes its coverage; past 55% a second set turned a third of a turn crosses it (square to the first, it read as a weave); their flow is smoothed more than the lines' and turns toward 45° where the picture is calm), Dots (stippling, `dotOf`, each cell's dot found once per patch: one dot per cell of a jittered grid, there where the darkness passes the cell's rank in blue noise, `SkissaFewer::blueNoise`, so dots never clump; small near their rank, growing past touching at the darkest; how much the grown dots cover is measured once, `dotsFor`, so the tone stays where they overlap), Screen (a print screen: the Euclidean dot on a turned grid, its level found from the dot's own area so coverage is exact, `spotLevel`; with Colors, cyan, magenta and yellow screens at their own angles under the ink's black: pop art), Engrave (parallel lines bent by the picture's lightness blurred at their spacing, each as wide as the share of its spacing that is dark), Contour (lines where the darkness passes each of Levels steps, bolder where darker); both count the line beyond the nearest (`bandsOver`), or the darkest never close. Screen and Engrave cover the tone as print does (coverage 1 − (L/level)³), the others lighter, as drawing does. Marks take their darkness evenly from the picture's lightness below the level, and lines stay whole over them: the edges alone, as they would be drawn on the paper's white (1 + edges against the level); added to the picture's own tone, they showed only where it was lighter than the level, and vanished under every mark. Every threshold is averaged over its pixel from how much D changes across it (`inkOver`, the tanh's integral), so edges are smooth however hard; marks are fixed to the image's pixels. Where the ink goes: Paper, Clear (line art pulled off its paper), Picture (over it), Colors (the picture's colors as ink, deepened as pigment laid twice). Tried and left: hatching as a percentage mixed with flat ink, Flow switching hatching to strokes, rows appearing by hidden rules (all read as confusing); straight lines widening with the tone as hatching (a mechanical screen; it is Screen and Engrave's look, where print is meant); thresholding noise smeared only along the flow (bands and blotches; it needs the band-pass across it); random dashes along hatch rows and white-noise dot ranks (both read as noise). Marks are drawn after the lines' smoothing, not smoothed with them. Looks as tiles of the layer's busiest part at half size, two rows of six (drawn with lines and tone: Lines, Ink, Comic, Pencil, Charcoal, Wash; with marks: Hatching, Etching, Stipple, Engraving, Halftone, Pop art; the owner found three rows too many; the shading kinds in one row of eight, so the panel fits), made off the window's thread after a pause, as are the shading and placement tiles; a drag on the image: up for more shading, sideways for size. 8 MP in about 0.4 s of work (Strokes more). It retires Krita's Halftone (its diamond, square and wave shapes aren't carried; old masks keep Krita's panel).
- **Fewer colors effect** (`SkissaFewer` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`): each pixel takes the nearest of a few colors in Oklab (`Quantizer`): steps of each channel on the sRGB curve (posterize, up to 32), grays even in Oklab lightness, colors found in the layer (k-means++ in Oklab on a thumbnail, done by the panel) or a palette's; the image's and palette's colors are kept in the settings, so patches, masks and previews agree. Lightness moves where the steps fall (Threshold is two grays). Pattern (Bayer) and Noise (void-and-cluster blue noise, made once) are Knoll's pattern dithering: 16 candidates that average to the pixel in linear light, picked by the threshold, fixed to the image's pixels. Diffuse (serpentine Floyd and Steinberg, error in linear light) runs through the whole image once and every patch takes its part: the result is found again by a key of the original pixels and the settings (`SkissaPixels::originalKey`), since a mask's patches each get a copy of their own. Pixel size above 1 is pixel art (`cellColors`): cells fixed to the image's corner, each one color, then fewer colors and dithering per cell (a pattern by cell, diffusion through the cells of the whole image). A cell is in the picture or out of it (alpha 0 or 1). A cell of one color takes its average; a cell of two (two means in Oklab with alpha) takes the larger, or the other where it stands out from the eight cells around and is more than a fifth of it, so thin lines and the dark side of edges stay whole instead of blending into a color in neither (a local stand-in for Kopf et al.'s content-adaptive downscaling, which optimizes each cell's footprint over the whole image). It retires Posterize, Threshold, Palettize, Index colors and Pixelize. Colors found in a layer aren't carried to the next by the last used settings (`lastUsedConfiguration`).
- **Shape effect** (`SkissaShape` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`, id `skissa_shape`): the layer's shape from its exact distance field (Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher, two passes of lower envelopes of parabolas, keeping which pixel is nearest, `nearestOf`; not jump flooding, which is the GPU's approximation), the edge found between pixels from the nearest opaque pixel's alpha, so any width is smooth and costs the same; only a pixel beside the other side holds the edge (deeper in, paint less than solid is see-through paint), and alpha counts as a share of the layer's solid paint (`solid`, found by the panel: all but a hundredth of its painted pixels are at most that opaque), so a layer painted at 40% still has a shape. Grow (positive grows, its new pixels the nearest edge's color: bolder line art; negative shrinks), Outline (Outside under the shape's soft edge, 3 px in, so the two meet without a seam; under the whole shape it tinted see-through paint; Center and Inside over it), Glow (a Gaussian falloff from the silhouette's edge), Shadow (the silhouette, the outside outline in it, moved and blurred; none at distance 0; Under, or Inside: what is around the shape moved into it and laid only over it, a cut-in look), Bevel (the edge as a rounded slope, a quarter of a sine rising inward over Size, its steepest Depth × 80°; Emboss puts it across the edge, its outer half shading what is around as black or white over it, since that may be on layers below; its normal leans along the distance field's gradient, read from the field smoothed by 1.5 px since exact distances step by whole pixels' roots and streaked round edges; lit as outdoors, each term as it differs from a flat surface's so the flat keeps the paint as it is: the sun that casts the shadow from 30° up (Lambert), the sky (a hemisphere, 0.4 of the sun) and a gloss (Blinn-Phong); the paint's own color is lit, where blending toward white and black turned a red bevel pink). Each is a tab with a dot while on, its right click resetting that part (as Color's and Light's tabs do: `KisStudioChips::rightClicked`); the bevel shades paint by multiplying and lights it by screening toward white, so a dark paint's bevel shows, and its soft edge pixels are on the slope too (left flat they drew a line of the paint's own color); looks combine them (Sticker: white outline and soft shadow; Neon; Button: bevel and soft shadow; Cut in: the shadow inside), five across. A drag on the image moves the shadow in its tab, elsewhere sideways is the part's size.
- **Light effect** (`SkissaLight` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`, id `skissa_light`): bright parts scattering light, added in linear light. What shines is brighter (cube root of luminance) than Threshold × the layer's brightest parts (`top`, as Radial blur), with a soft knee, and the whitest parts count as the clipped light they stand for (an inverse Reinhard curve, up to ten times; not in float images, which hold their light): without it an 8-bit sun barely glowed. That is `SkissaPixels::shineOf`, shared with Radial blur's rays; Lens's glow spreads the same unclipped light (`unclipOf`). Saturated colors don't shine by themselves: pure paint can't be told from a light, and painted lights have white cores. Glow is glare falling off about as r^-2.6 (Gaussians from 1.5 px to a third of Size, evenly by ratio at most an octave apart, each weighed by that power and the share of the range it stands for: fixed octaves made Size move in jumps); Star is an aperture's diffraction (Blades, Length, Angle): each straight blade edge spreads light in a line square to it, so an even count makes as many spikes and an odd count twice as many (`blades`; settings from before held the even `points`), falling off as 1/r² (fades of every length f, each as bright as 1/f², sum to exactly 1/r²; three a ratio of 3 apart stand for them, at a tenth, three tenths and all of Length), each wavelength's a different length so the ends turn red; Streak is an anamorphic line in its own color; Halation is film's red halo: the light's own color times what of each color reaches the film's base (`halationTint`: red nearly all, blue almost none past the yellow filter; so cyan light makes almost none), spread as light scattered in the emulsion and reflected by the base falls, (1 + (r/2t)²)^-2, made as Gaussians weighted e^(-a²/2σ²)/σ² (`addGlare` takes a weight per size; the glow's is a power). Looks: None, Bloom, Film, Star, one row. Arbitrary aperture masks (eyelashes, dust) would need the PSF as the Fourier transform of the pupil, which isn't there. Spikes are made on a grid fixed to the image's pixels (64 cells along a spike) and enlarged; their fade is a recursive filter run both ways along the rows of the grid turned to the spike's direction (`addSpikes`), so a spike of any length costs a few passes (summing each spike's samples took seconds). The glow's octaves are blurred on the coarsest level of a pyramid of the shine that still holds two cells of them, cells fixed to the image's corner. A huge bright area makes haze, not a star, as a lens does. 8 MP in about 0.25 s.
- **Painting effect** (`SkissaPaint` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`, id `skissa_paint`): the anisotropic Kuwahara filter (Kyprianidis, Kang and Döllner) with polynomial sector weights (Kyprianidis 2011): an ellipse along the flow of the structure tensor of its Oklab colors and coverage (of lightness alone, edges between equally light colors and white shapes on a clear layer had none), eight overlapping sectors, each pixel the sectors' means weighed toward the calmest (1 / (1 + (1000 σ²)^(q/2)), in Oklab with alpha). The sector weights put the disk at radius 1 (at a half, as some shaders scale it, every sector covered everything and the result was a plain blur). The ellipse is read at 48 sunflower points, precomputed with the sectors they count in, each at its nearest pixel (bilinear reads cost twice as much and looked the same), and the sector weights' power is a rough one (`roughPow`); past a radius of 8 the picture is blurred first by a tenth of it. Size, Sharpness, Flow. It covers Krita's Oil paint. 8 MP in about 0.55 s.
- **Lens blur** (`SkissaFocus` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`, id `skissa_focus`): the picture out of focus as a lens shows it, each point spread into the aperture's shape (round, or a polygon of 5, 6 or 8 blades, as tiles of the iris), in linear light. Highlights first become the light they stand for (`SkissaPixels::unclipOf`) and after the blur go back through its inverse (`reclipOf`), so whatever didn't spread comes back byte for byte while lights spread into bright discs; clipping instead of going back turned every pale surface white. Each point spreads its light into the aperture at its own size (scatter, as light out of focus does), and a pixel is the light reaching it over the weights reaching it, so nothing brightens or darkens where sizes change and the image's edges (nothing past them spreads) stay whole. A point writes where each row of its aperture starts and ends, partly covered end pixels by their share (doubles: in floats a bright row's sum drowned the darks), and the rows are run through once; points whose aperture is wider than 16 cells spread on grids of 2^n px fixed to the image's corner, read back between the cells' middles. Patches match the whole image exactly. Even, or Tilt-shift: in focus along a band through a middle (placed by a press on the image), at Angle, Width a share of the half diagonal, the blur growing to Size at the farthest corner. Mixing blurs of a few even sizes instead made two rings around every light between them. It retires Krita's Lens blur, which blurred in gamma (dark fringes, dead highlights); its panel stays for old masks.
- **Effect panels** (the owner's ask: simple, minimal, obvious): what an effect can do as pictures of the layer, each setting a `KisStudioValueRow` (drag, a click on the bar sets the value there, `setClickSetsValue`, double-click on the number types, right-click resets; never Krita's slider boxes, whose whole-range switch read as clutter), one per line, and a drag on the image for the main value. Fewer colors: where the colors come from as pictures of the whole layer (Picture, Gray, Palette; Levels only while a setting uses it), Colors, Pixel size, Brightness, dither ramps, Amount. A drag on the image follows one rule: up or down is the effect's amount, sideways its size, and a press places the middle where there is one (Lens, Radial blur); Detail, Noise and Color's curves go up or down only. The effects of a slider or two (`SkissaSliders`: Blur, Sharpen, High pass, Smooth) are canvas editors too: up or down their Amount (or their only size), sideways their size; the overlay's own first-numbers drag finds only Krita's spin boxes and sliders. The pointer over the image says which ways (`KisEffectCanvasEditor::canvasAxes`: both, up or down, or a crosshair for a middle), and the values show in a pill from the press on. Directions are `KisStudioValueRow`s with `Range::angle`: a dial before the number, turned by dragging around it (`dialTurn` when the value names the direction across what it is about, as Drawing's angle does).
- **Replace color** (`SkissaReplaceFilter` in `plugins/filters/skissa_detail`, id `skissa_replace`, its panel `replaceColor` in `KisEffectPanels`): one color made another or see-through (With's alpha), a pixel holding some of it read as the color with paint over it, that paint laid over the new color (`replaceShare`), so soft edges keep their blend; on the values as the image keeps them, which Krita blended in. Two ways, as tiles: Behind all (the color under everything, GIMP's color to alpha, `shareUnder`; mixes of colors that cancel to gray aren't read as the color) and Just this color (`shareBeside`: only the color and its blends, found by walking the picture's slope up to 64 px each way: downhill it must come to the color without crossing an even patch over 8 px, uphill the blend's other end). Plain color to alpha for a fill color read skin as a third red and turned the gray edges of black lines teal; a fixed ring of neighbours left soft strokes half done with a blocky ring. A press picks the color and the way: Background when the color is 30% of the layer or more. Range (drag up or down on the image) is what counts as the color; a hairline that never reaches its color needs it raised. It retires Color to alpha.
- **Light past white**: an 8-bit picture is taken to have been made by Reinhard's curve with white at 10× white's light (`SkissaPixels::whiteLight`); `unclipOf` undoes it, `reclipOf` redoes it. One guess for every effect that spreads light (Light, Lens blur, Lens's glow, Radial blur's rays); float images hold their light.
- **Hidden colors** (`KisHiddenColors`): erasing lowers only alpha, so clear pixels keep what was erased; PNG export writes them cleared (`KisPNGConverter::buildFile`), as GIMP does, and Export's sizes count without them. WebP drops them itself; .kra keeps them.
- **Panels for Krita's effects** (`KisEffectPanels`, asked for by `KisFilterSelectorWidget::setFilter` before the filter's own; Halftone, Pixelize, Gradient map, Color to alpha, Lens blur and Propagate colors (Shape's Grow) are retired and Height to normal map left out; their panels stay for old masks): Halftone (shapes, Paper/Clear/Color, Size, Angle, Softness, Ink and Paper), Desaturate (its ways as pictures; Ctrl+Shift+U opens it), Motion blur (a drag on the image draws the motion), Lens blur (iris shapes drawn), Pixelize, Color to alpha (a click on the image picks the color), Propagate colors, Height to normal map, Gradient map (looks, a bar of stops: drag, click adds, right click removes; Smooth/Steps/Dither). Each writes Krita's settings as Krita's own panel does (Halftone's screens are flat `<screen>_generator_screentone_<key>` properties; Lens blur's `halfWidth`/`halfHeight` follow the iris), so files and masks stay Krita's. A `Panel` is tiles (`Pictures::Detail` a busy part at its own size, `Whole` the layer small, `None` words; `forPictures` changes what only the tiles' pictures use, as Halftone's screen kept to a third of a tile), value rows, colors and switches; pictures run the filter itself on a copy, which needs `cloneWithResourcesSnapshot` and the layer's default bounds (Krita's blurs assert without them).
- **A preview never shows the layer half done.** A single layer (or a group's only child) is the image's projection itself (`KisGroupLayer::tryObligeChild`), and the canvas reads the projection right after each merge, on the merge's thread (`sigImageUpdated` is a direct connection). So in `kis_filter_stroke_strategy.cpp` everything that writes or takes back the layer while merges may run waits for them first (barrier jobs) or asks for no update until it is done (`disableDirtyRequests`): the coarse pass taken back on cancel and before the full pass, the coarse pass written. A dragged Blur flickered to the unblurred image at about every step before. To test it, read pixels on the merge's thread like the canvas; a queued slot reads later and sees states the canvas never shows.
- **Preview passes** (`kis_filter_stroke_strategy.cpp`, `coarsePass`): a coarse pass at once (enlarged with colors weighted by alpha, `KisCoarseFilterPreview::upscale`, or clear pixels darken the edges beside them), then (zoomed out with Krita's instant preview off) one at the canvas's own resolution (`displayLevel`), then the full pass after a short settle (`qBound(40, estimate / 8, 400)` ms); each is written over the one before in one step. Zoomed out with instant preview on, Krita's own level-of-detail stroke previews instead.
- **Pictures that show little**: an effect's pictures (the picker's, a panel's tiles, Detail, Noise, Lens, Color) use the sample painting (or Detail's zone plate) when the layer's picture shows too little to see an effect on (`KisEffectPanels::showsLittle`: what differs from the commonest color, thinned 2 px from every side, under a twelfth of it left): thin line art on clear or on paper. Fewer colors keeps the layer: its colors come from it.
- **Filter patches** are sized from how far the filter reads around them (`neededRect`, `patchSizeFor` in `kis_filter_stroke_strategy.cpp`): with 512 px patches a wide blur read 15 times its area for each.
- **Effects that read the whole image** (Lens, Radial blur, Fewer colors' diffusion) say so (`KisFilter::readsWholeImage`); masks, filter layers and `KisFilter::process` ask with the image's bounds (`neededRectIn`/`changedRectIn`, which give the rect and the image). Their own `neededRect`, not told where the image ends, adds 16384 px each side: masks under them worked over 33,000 px squares, never settled (saving said "Image is busy", undo hung) and Motion blur's FFT asked for 100 GB and crashed. Such a mask makes every small update of its layer redo the whole image below it, so the update queue drops a job whose work a queued one already covers (`covers` in `kis_simple_update_queue.cpp`). A failed FFT allocation throws `std::bad_alloc`, which `KisFilter::process` reports.
- **Effects that read the whole frame** (Lens, Radial blur): every patch of a run, and every patch of a mask (each a copy of the same pixels), takes its part of one result made over the whole image (`SkissaPixels::wholeResult`, keyed by the pixels as they were and the settings, made in bands on every core; up to 16 MP, past that each patch makes its own). Not at a level of detail (`currentLevelOfDetail`), whose device holds a smaller picture.
- **Effects read the pixels as they were** (`SkissaPixels::readOriginal`, the transaction's old data): patches run side by side in one layer, and `readBytes` would read a neighbour already written.
- **Effects follow transforms** (`libs/image/KisFilterGeometry`, `plugins/filters/skissa_detail/SkissaFollow`): scaling, turning, shearing or mirroring the image or a layer changes its filter masks' and filter layers' settings with it, in the same undo step, so they look the same: lengths scale by the square root of the area's growth (Detail's and Noise's size curves shift along their axis), directions turn (and flip when mirrored, Radial blur's spin too), a blur stretched one way stays across and down, middles kept as shares of the image move with it. Each filter says how its settings follow (`KisFilterGeometry::add`; Krita's in `KisFilterGeometry.cpp`, Skissa's in `SkissaFollow::addAll`): a new effect with sizes, angles or places goes there. Krita's transform and mirror visitors call `Follower::follow`, once per node (a node is visited for each of its frames); transforming a selected part leaves settings alone. Fill layers (generators, Halftone's screens) don't follow yet.
- **Baking a filter mask**: Merge down (Ctrl+E) on filter masks writes each one's effect into its paint layer's pixels where it lets it through, the mask gone, one step (`KisStudioEffectBrush::bake`, from `KisLayerManager::mergeLayer`); Krita merged the mask's layer down instead. On a group or vector layer it says why it can't.
