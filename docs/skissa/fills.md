# Fills

Part of CLAUDE.md, moved out to keep every session's context small. Read it before working in this area; keep it current.

- **Fills** (`libs/image/floodfill/KisLineArtFill`, `KisColorDrop`, `KisColorAreas`, `plugins/tools/basictools/kis_tool_fill.cc`):
  - `KisLineArtFill::grow` runs after Krita's flood fill (`KisFillPainter::setLineArt`): a watershed so the fill reaches under the lines to where they meet the next area, taking in soft edges and specks. A fill over 24 MP grows by tiles of 4096 px, each reading 128 px around it from the fill as the flood made it. The Fill tool has Under lines and Specks. Layer → Auto Flats colors every enclosed area (`KisLineArtFill::flats`).
  - Ink is one measure (`KisLineArtFill::inkOf`: darkness × coverage) and a pixel with `lineInk` (64) or more is a line, for the drop's choice of layer, flats and filling under lines alike; only line widths are measured on the line's core (96).
  - Every fill (a drop, the Fill tool, Enclose and Fill) chooses its layer alike (`KisColorDrop::fillTarget`): on line art a "Colors" layer (below clear line art, Multiply above opaque). A gap of 0 is Auto, sized from the line width (`autoGap`). What counts as a line is dark ink, not earlier fills (`isColorsLayer`). A Colors layer is marked by the node property `KisColorDrop::colorsMark`, saved in .kra as the layer attribute `skissacolors` (upstream ignores it), so a renamed one or one named in another language still takes fills; layers from before the mark are known by their name.
  - A drop on line art stops only at the lines: the picture is flooded with everything below `lineInk` made clear (`linesOnly`, made in the preview's worker and in the fill's stroke), so faint sketch lines and smudges under the line art don't split an area; a drop on a line itself floods the picture as it is. Its panel shows no Spread there (only fills by color have one). The Fill tool and Enclose and Fill keep Krita's boundary; with "Current layer" on an empty layer or a Colors layer they read the picture, as a drop does (an empty layer has no lines, so it filled everything).
  - Auto gap (`gapFor`): twice the width of the lines near the drop (4 to 12 px), read from a piece of the image, so a click a little elsewhere can read thinner lines; a fill from inside that still reaches the image's edge went through a wider opening, so Auto doubles the gap (to 32) until the fill stays off the edges and at most half as large, else keeps the first. That search floods several times: it runs only in the preview's worker, and the drop and every refill reuse the gap it found (`previewGap`, `dropGap`, `autoChosen`); flooding on the window's thread hung Skissa on a Spread drag.
  - Fills by color (`ByColor`, `byColorAt`, `fillByColor`), with no gap or filling under lines, and a panel of Spread and, beside ✓ and ✕, an Everywhere toggle (`studio_everywhere`, drops here and there, solid while on; a checkbox row read as out of place). Line art (its ink, on the layer or as the picture shows, mostly in runs 32 px or thinner, `Look::lines`; Krita's ink counts any darker color, so red shapes were ink) keeps the lines' fill; anything else is by color: the active layer's own paint under the drop recolored in place, a clear spot painted over as the picture is seen. Decided by the picture, not the layer: a layer half filled by drops was neither a painting nor clear and went the line-art way.
  - The area is one model (`KisColorAreas`). The step at a pixel is how far apart the picture's colors are on its two sides (Oklab, premultiplied, smoothed by a Gaussian of 1 px), read 3, 5, 7 and 9 px out and the least taken, in the direction it is most: an edge is a step at every distance, a thin line only close up, so lines don't part what they lie on, and a crease or slight gradient is no step (a gradient measure through the Gaussian's derivative split the sunset's water at its faint reflection lines, which were steeper than the water's faint edge with the land; removing the straight gradient (2D(r) − D(2r)) took 6–8 px stripes for lines). The area under the pointer at every step at once is grown from its most inside pixel in order of step (a priority flood: the pixels taken before the first past a level are the area at that level), stopping once the stable level is known; a min-tree of the whole picture (Berger's union-find) gave the same and cost 0.3 s on one thread. For a given step the area is the one most of the pointer's two screen pixels cover. Spread is the step, 0.0025 a unit; each drop finds its own (`autoStep`): the first level, from half the least noticeable difference (0.01: eight-bit color makes slight gradients flat bands a level apart, each stable) up, where the area's size changes least, a local least under 0.25 over a span of 5 levels either way (MSER's usual), a quarter into its run (toward its top the area is closer to spilling into its neighbour; the middle overfilled); the longest run instead took water and land together. The panel shows it; a drag changes it for that fill only, right click goes back to it, and the line fills' tolerance isn't written. Everywhere takes the pieces whose mean color and spread of colors are each within the step of the dropped-on one's. Edge pixels are followed out to 32 px to the nearest pixel of the area and of another area; their colors are read where they stop changing away from each other (`further`: the nearest whole pixels of a soft edge are still part blended, which left a gray ring and old-color corners), and the pixel is unmixed between them by least squares in the picture's own encoding, trusted as far as it explains the pixel (span² / (span² + off-line²)), the rest of the share from where it lies between the two (a seam darker than either, where two near alike shapes overlapped, read as no share and left dashed dark outlines). An edge pixel enclosed in the area, no other area near, is the area within the step of its color, else a mark. Marks on the area (a pixel a step from the color across the line it is on, `lineLevel`; a bird, a faint line, a seam) take the new color shifted by their difference from the area beside them; enclosed lines kept as their own color showed old-color streaks on the new one. Thin areas enclosed by the one filled, absorbed as marks, were tried: measured after their blurred edge is eaten away, a faint 3 px line was as thick as the stripes, which then changed color.
  - Speed (4K): the steps once per picture (about 0.2 s on every core, distances compared squared), and after a fill again only where the picture changed (rows compared with the reading before, `before`), an auto step and area 30–190 ms, Everywhere about 160 ms (areas labelled, then summed on every core), the fill 30 ms. Hovering shows the picture as it will look (`previewOf`; line art the region in solid color); a drop takes over what its preview worked out. Spread and Everywhere changes work the area out in the background from the picture before the fill, one at a time, each finished one filled while the newest waiting value is worked out next: throwing a finished one away whenever a newer value waited showed nothing during a drag. Nothing floods on the window's thread.
  - A color dragged onto the canvas (rail chip, swatches, palette, pinned colors) previews the area under the pointer; after the drop a panel by it sets the gap (Auto shows the size it chose), Spread (both value rows) and Under lines (refilling) and keeps filling (`KeepFillingChip`). The panel stays until the fill is decided: ✓ or Enter keeps it, ✕ or Esc takes it back, a plain press on the canvas (painting on) or another drop keeps it, while panning, zooming and turning (other buttons, modifiers, Space) leave it open; a timeout that closed it left people not knowing how to finish. Spread's row is by ratios. Holding still mid-drag to tune Spread was tried and taken out: artists pause while choosing where to drop, and the fill stayed where they paused. Spread is shared with the Fill tool's Threshold setting: for line art it is that color tolerance, for fills by color the edge step above; it is not Krita's opacity spread. With a modifier held, Krita's own color drop runs. In a selection, or within a few screen pixels of its outline, a drop fills the whole selection, shown whole while dragging (`onSelection`). A color dropped on a layer's picture in the layer panel recolors its paint, keeping its transparency (a group's: its visible paint layers), shown on the canvas and in the picture while it is held there; on the rest of the row it gives the row the nearest color label (OKLab), undoable; between rows it makes a fill layer (`plugins/dockers/skissa_layers/LayerColorDrop`, `KisColorDrop::recolor`). Krita's own list recolors on the whole row (`KisNodeModel::dropMimeData`).
  - A fill through `FillProcessingVisitor` needs a `KisUpdateCommand` with its dirty rect in the same stroke or the canvas doesn't update.
- **One Fill tool** (`KisToolFill::finishDrag`): a click fills the area under it; a drag draws a line instead (the click's fill taken back with `cancelStroke`), and when it ends within 40 screen px of where it began it is a loop, filled inside as Enclose and Fill does (`KisEncloseAndFillProcessingVisitor`, all regions, on the layer and with the gap every fill chooses); an open line fills each area it crossed (Krita's drag fill, or only where it began with that off). Enclose and Fill left the Fill slot; Find still has it.
- **Pattern Tint** (`KisPatternTint`, `StudioPatternTint`): a switch in the Fill tool's pattern popup; while on, every fill that takes the pattern (through `KisResourcesSnapshot`) paints it in the current color, its darkness as the paint, so any texture fills in any color. Made once per pattern and color.
